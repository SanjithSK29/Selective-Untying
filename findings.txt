================================================================================
1. RESEARCH QUESTION
================================================================================

Weight tying shares the token embedding between input encoding and output
prediction. Ref [3] (Lopardo et al., Findings of ACL 2026, arXiv 2603.26663)
shows that in tied models the shared embedding is dominated early in training
by the OUTPUT-side gradient, biasing it toward output prediction ("unembedding
bias").

Hypothesis: selectively adding output-side offsets (Delta_i) to tokens with
strong input/output gradient CONFLICT could repair this bias — cheaper and more
targeted than full untying.

Success criterion (pre-registered): conflict selection counts as positive ONLY
if mean all-keyword satisfaction is >= 2 percentage points above BOTH the
frequency and frequency-matched-random baselines across 3 paired seeds, with no
mean degradation in response NLL vs the tied baseline. Otherwise: report as
null/mixed.


================================================================================
2. SETUP
================================================================================

Model (decoder-only, tied embeddings):
  - 6 pre-LayerNorm blocks, d_model=384, 6 heads (head_dim 64)
  - GELU FF width 1536 (4x), learned positional embeddings
  - 8000-token byte-level BPE vocabulary, context length 256
  - 13,817,856 parameters (tied); 16,889,856 (fully untied, +3.07M)

Data:
  - Pretraining: TinyStories (train.csv, ~1.9 GB, ~470M tokens)
  - SFT/calibration: TinyStoriesInstruct (train 21.8M records, valid 218k)

Hardware: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (8 GB), bf16 mixed precision.


================================================================================
3. PRETRAINING RESULTS
================================================================================

  Steps: 28,503 (one full pass, ~470M tokens)
  Train loss: 8.9 -> 3.02 (smooth, no divergence)
  Validation loss: 1.466   ->  perplexity 4.33

================================================================================
4. CALIBRATION (gradient-conflict measurement)
================================================================================

Method: two-pass gradient decomposition on the tied checkpoint.
  g_total = g_in + g_out (autograd sums them on the shared weight)
  g_out = obtained by detaching the input path
  g_in  = g_total - g_out
  conflict[i] = -cosine_similarity(g_in[i], g_out[i])

  Calibration tokens: 10,000,000 (SFT training stories, <=256 tokens)
  Eligibility: freq >= 20, non-special token, both gradient norms > 0
  Eligible rows: 6,341
  Selected rows: 400 (cap = min(400, 10% of eligible))
  Top conflict scores: 0.52, 0.45, 0.43, 0.42, 0.41, 0.40, 0.40, 0.40

The conflict signal is REAL and measurable: scores are positive and well
separated from 0 (mean 0.4-0.5 among the selected tail).


================================================================================
5. CONFLICT-SIGNAL ANALYSIS (is it just frequency?)
================================================================================

Distribution (over 6,341 eligible tokens):
  mean conflict: -0.021, median -0.017, min -0.575, max +0.520
  fraction with conflict > 0.3: 1.0%  (signal is a concentrated tail)
  fraction with conflict < 0.0: 55.4%

Conflict vs frequency (the key methodological question):
  Pearson  correlation: +0.015
  Spearman (rank) corr:  +0.112   <-- essentially orthogonal to frequency

Conflict vs gradient magnitude:
  Spearman (total norm) : -0.024
  Spearman (input norm) : +0.030
  Spearman (output norm): +0.040   <-- conflict is directional, not magnitude

Selection overlap (Jaccard, 400 rows each):
  conflict vs frequency-selected : 0.022
  conflict vs grad-norm-selected : 0.023

Top tokens by conflict (decoded): character names and concrete nouns, e.g.
  Jacob, Claire, Jenna, Luke, Harry, Tilly, Mira, Sunny,
  butterfly, cupcakes, cardboard, jewel, bone, pond, explore, prepare

Top tokens by frequency (for contrast): function words
  '.', ',', 'the', 'and', 'to', 'a', 'was', 'it', 'her', 'said'

FINDING: the conflict selector is a genuinely DIFFERENT and interpretable
selector from frequency — it picks character names and rare content words, not
function words. Conflict is orthogonal to both frequency (rho=0.11) and
gradient norm (rho=-0.02).


================================================================================
6. SFT RESULTS — THE 6 METHODS (3 seeds each)
================================================================================

Methods: tied, full (untying), conflict, frequency, freq_random, grad_norm.
All selective methods add the same 400 output offsets (Delta init 0).

Mean keyword satisfaction and response NLL (3 seeds):

  method        mean satisfaction   mean NLL     (per-seed satisfaction)
  ------------  ------------------  -----------  --------------------------------
  freq_random   0.347               1.5084       [0.370, 0.312, 0.360]
  conflict      0.345               1.5088       [0.368, 0.308, 0.360]
  tied          0.344               1.5074       [0.366, 0.296, 0.370]
  frequency     0.341               1.5071       [0.338, 0.314, 0.370]
  grad_norm     0.340               1.5065       [0.344, 0.298, 0.378]
  full          0.331               1.5048       [0.346, 0.286, 0.360]

Paired differences (conflict vs baselines), per seed:
  conflict - frequency   : [+0.030, -0.006, -0.010]  mean +0.0047 (+0.47pp)
  conflict - freq_random : [-0.002, -0.004, +0.000]  mean -0.0020 (-0.20pp)
  conflict - tied        : [+0.002, +0.012, -0.010]  mean +0.0013 (+0.13pp)

VERDICT (vs pre-registered criterion):
  conflict - frequency   = +0.47pp  (need >= 2.0pp)  -> FAIL
  conflict - freq_random = -0.20pp  (need >= 2.0pp)  -> FAIL
  => RESULT: NULL / MIXED. Selective untying has no detectable effect.

Statistical note: seed-to-seed spread is ~4pp, while between-method differences
are ~0.2-0.5pp — the intervention effect is ~10x smaller than the noise. The
study is underpowered to resolve a 2pp effect with 3 seeds; but the effect is
also simply tiny (and inconsistent across seeds).


================================================================================
7. INPUT-GRADIENT SCALING (ref [3]'s causal fix) — DOSE-RESPONSE
================================================================================

Instead of untying, scale the INPUT-path gradient by alpha during SFT:
  dL/dW_effective = alpha * g_in + g_out
alpha = 1 is exactly the tied baseline. Swept alpha in {1, 2, 5, 10}, 3 seeds.

  alpha   mean NLL   mean satisfaction
  ------  ---------  -----------------
  1.0     1.5074     0.344
  2.0     1.5068     0.342
  5.0     1.5054     0.335
  10.0    1.5040     0.328

Two MONOTONIC, opposite dose-response trends:

  NLL:          1.5074 -> 1.5040  (improves, -0.0034)
                 HIGHLY SIGNIFICANT: seed SD is only 0.0001, so 0.0034 is ~34 SDs.

  satisfaction: 0.344 -> 0.328    (degrades, -1.6pp)
                 Monotonic across all 4 points (suggestive; per-point seed SD ~3pp).

FINDING: input-gradient scaling (the fix ref [3] actually proposed) produces a
real, statistically significant, monotonic effect at 13.8M scale — but it is a
TRADEOFF, not a win: it improves next-token prediction (NLL) while degrading
instruction-following (keyword satisfaction).


================================================================================
8. SYNTHESIS / CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

1. Selective (per-token) untying: NULL. 400 rows (~1.1% of params) produce no
   detectable change in keyword satisfaction. The effect does not meet the
   pre-registered 2pp criterion and is ~10x smaller than seed noise.

2. The conflict signal itself is real and interpretable: it is orthogonal to
   frequency (rho=0.11) and gradient norm (rho=-0.02), selects character names
   and concrete nouns (not function words), and has near-zero Jaccard overlap
   with frequency selection (0.022). Measuring it is a genuine methodological
   contribution.

3. Both interventions that address the "unembedding bias" — full untying AND
   input-gradient scaling — push the model in the SAME direction: better
   next-token prediction (lower NLL) but WORSE instruction-following (lower
   keyword satisfaction). Full untying: best NLL (1.5048), worst satisfaction
   (0.331). Gradient scaling (alpha=10): best NLL (1.5040), worst satisfaction
   (0.328).

   Mechanistic reading: reducing the output's hold on the embedding makes it
   more "input-representation-like" (ref [3]'s goal), which helps natural
   next-token prediction — but at the cost of faithfully following the
   keyword-constrained instruction.

4. The cleanest positive result in the project is the DOSE-RESPONSE tradeoff:
   the unembedding bias is real and manipulable at 13.8M scale (via global
   gradient rebalancing), but token-level untying is the wrong granularity to
   capture it, and the bias itself is a fluency/instruction-following tradeoff
   rather than a pure defect.


================================================================================
9. LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

  - Single pretrained checkpoint; findings conditional on it.
  - 3 seeds => underpowered for a 2pp behavioral effect (seed SD ~4pp).
  - 13.8M model on TinyStories: small scale, short-context, constrained story
    generation; results may not transfer to larger models or other tasks.
  - Keyword satisfaction is a coarse binary metric (all-words-present); it is
    dominated by the transformer blocks, not the embedding.
  - Coherence / keyword-integration ratings (blinded human) not yet run.
  - SFT hyperparameters (20k examples, 1000 steps, lr 1e-4) are reasonable but
    untuned.
  - No removal of eval-story overlaps from training corpora was implemented.
